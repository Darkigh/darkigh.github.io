📊 Conclusions & Analysis

Conclusion 1 — E-commerce is mainstream among young adults. 85% of surveyed people (17 out of 20) have purchased something online, and 50% of respondents were aged 18–25. This confirms findings from the Pew Internet & American Life Project, which reports that over 80% of American internet users have made an online purchase, with younger adults being the most frequent shoppers.

Conclusion 2 — Monthly shopping is the most common habit. 40% of respondents shop online once a month, while 30% shop once a week — showing that regular online purchasing is now a normal part of life for most people. According to eMarketer, global e-commerce sales have grown consistently year over year, driven largely by convenience, competitive pricing, and mobile accessibility.

Conclusion 3 — Amazon dominates, but alternatives are growing. Amazon was chosen by 45% of respondents as their favorite shopping site. However, platforms like AliExpress (20%) and Temu (10%) are gaining popularity, especially among price-conscious younger shoppers. ClickZ data shows that marketplace diversification is one of the key trends in modern e-commerce.

Conclusion 4 — Education level correlates with online shopping activity. The majority of frequent shoppers (once a week or month) were in the "some college" or "college graduate" categories. Those with only a high school education tended to shop less frequently, which may reflect differences in income, internet confidence, or digital access.
